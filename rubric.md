# Interview practice scoring rubric

Score each dimension from 1 to 5 using whole numbers. Score only what the candidate actually said, including at most one follow-up answer. Use the question's `ideal_points` as the content checklist. The same anchors apply across sessions.

| Dimension | Weight | What to assess |
|---|---:|---|
| Relevance & Accuracy | 30% | Addresses the question and is factually or technically correct |
| Structure & Clarity | 25% | Easy to follow, logically ordered, appropriately concise; STAR is useful for behavioral answers |
| Depth & Specificity | 25% | Concrete details, reasoning, examples, numbers or trade-offs when relevant |
| Communication & Confidence | 10% | Clear, direct, professional wording without unnecessary filler; never judge accent or identity |
| Impact & Reflection | 10% | Result, lesson, ownership, or practical consequence appropriate to the question |

| Score | Anchor |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Excellent; interview-ready answer with strong evidence |
| 4 | Strong; minor gaps |
| 3 | Adequate; correct but generic or uneven |
| 2 | Weak; partial, vague, or significant gaps |
| 1 | Off-target, incorrect, or nearly empty |

`overall = 0.30*relevance + 0.25*structure + 0.25*depth + 0.10*communication + 0.10*impact`, rounded to one decimal. Exact halfway values use ties-to-even rounding, matching the example in the supplied specification (3.65 → 3.6).

Bands: 4.5–5.0 Excellent; 3.5–4.4 Strong; 2.5–3.4 Developing; below 2.5 Needs work. The score measures this practice answer, not hiring chances. Do not reward length alone. If a technical detail cannot be verified, flag it and avoid claiming certainty.
